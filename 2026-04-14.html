Build the Sentence

"also" — where does it go?
• Before the main verb: She also speaks French.
• After be: He is also responsible.
• Between auxiliary and main verb: They have also finished.
• Never after the main verb: She speaks also French. ✗

"and I" vs "and me" — remove the other person to test:
• Subject → use I: My colleague and I attended. (“I attended” ✓)
• Object → use me: She thanked my colleague and me. (“She thanked me” ✓)

Adjectives go before the noun. Adverbs of manner go after the verb and its object.
2

Verb + Preposition

Verb + preposition collocations must be memorised — the preposition is often unexpected, and changing it can change the meaning completely.

False friends — Italian suggests the wrong preposition:
• pensare a → think about  (not think to)
• arrivare a → arrive at / in  (not arrive to)
• dipendere da → depend on  (not depend from)

Watch out — similar phrasal verbs, very different meanings:
• carry out (eseguire)  vs  carry on (continuare)
• result in (causare)  vs  result from (essere causato da)

5

Pronunciation — Read Aloud

Read each sentence out loud. Look up every highlighted word on wordreference.com before you start.

-ed endings — three pronunciations:
• /t/ after a voiceless consonant: worked, finished, increased
• /d/ after a voiced consonant or vowel: followed, reviewed, remained
• /ɪd/ after /t/ or /d/: completed, suggested, presented
-ed verbs
tricky pronunciation
She followed the correct procedures and completed the task as planned.
The whole team reviewed the budget and suggested several improvements.
He proved he was capable of managing a large volume of work.
The volume of complaints increased after the new procedures were introduced.
She identified the key issues, prepared a detailed report, and presented her findings to the whole team.

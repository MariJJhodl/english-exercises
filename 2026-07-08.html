5 exercises · Grammar & Vocabulary

1

Do or Make?

Do and make both translate the Italian fare, but English treats them as two separate verbs with fixed collocations — you cannot choose freely.

• do = general activity, effort, work: do research, do business, do damage, do your best, do everything possible
• make = create, produce, decide: make a decision, make a complaint, make progress, make sure, make an exception
2

Build the Sentence — Legal Vocabulary

Common mistake: putting the complement (adverb, time, place) between the verb and the object — a direct calque from Italian (“ha dichiarato immediatamente la legge…”).

English order is fixed: Subject + Verb + Object + Complement(s). The complement always comes after the object, never between the verb and the object.

“enforces strictly the regulations” ✗  →  “enforces the regulations strictly” ✓
3

Phrasal Verbs

New phrasal verbs today, common in business and legal contexts:

• draw up = prepare a formal document  •  rule out = eliminate a possibility
• point out = mention, indicate  •  work out = find a solution
• take on = accept responsibility for  •  turn down = reject an offer
• hand over = give control/documents to someone  •  come up with = produce an idea or solution
4

“In the sense that” — Choose the Best Alternative

“In the sense that” can often be replaced by a more precise formal alternative — but the right one depends on the logical relationship:

• in that = explains the specific reason/respect for a claim already made
• insofar as / to the extent that = introduce a limit or degree (true only up to a point)
• meaning that = introduces a consequence that follows from a fact

Watch out: namely introduces a specific name/example, and in other words restates the whole idea — neither works as a direct replacement.
5

Rewrite in a Formal Style

Read the informal text below (topic: law and legislation) and rewrite it in a more formal register — formal vocabulary, no contractions, no vague expressions like a lot of or maybe.

⚠️ Spelling reminder: the verb become is irregular — it becomes (never it becames), it’s becoming (never it’s becaming).
“A lot of people think the new law is a good idea, but some say it doesn’t work well in real life. The government needs to look into it more and maybe change a few things, because right now it’s causing problems for small businesses. If they don’t do something soon, it could become a much bigger issue for everyone.”
Alternative 1 Many stakeholders regard the new legislation favourably; however, some argue that it is not effective in practice. The government must examine the matter further and consider implementing amendments, as the current provisions are creating significant difficulties for small businesses. Failure to act promptly could result in a considerably more serious issue for the sector as a whole.
Alternative 2 While the new legislation has been welcomed by many, critics contend that it fails to achieve its intended outcomes in practice. It is therefore necessary for the government to review the matter and consider appropriate amendments, given that the current framework is generating substantial challenges for small businesses. Should no action be taken in the near future, the situation risks becoming considerably more problematic for all parties involved.

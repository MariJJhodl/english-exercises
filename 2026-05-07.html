1

Few, A Few, Little, A Little

few / little → not many / not much — negative meaning (not enough)
a few / a little → some — positive meaning (enough for something)

few / a few → countable nouns: friends, people, minutes, ideas
little / a little → uncountable nouns: time, money, advice, information, patience

Examples:
• I have a little time. (= some time, enough)  •  I have little time. (= almost none, not enough)
• She has a few friends. (= some, positive)  •  She has few friends. (= not many, negative)
2

Build the Sentence — Object Placement

In English, the direct object always goes directly after the verb — never after a complement or adverb.

• I find [receiving off-topic messages] very frustrating. ✓
• I find very frustrating receiving off-topic messages. ✗

When the object is a long clause (when…, to…, that…), use it as a placeholder directly after the verb, and put the real content at the end:
• I find it very frustrating when people arrive late. ✓
• She finds it difficult to concentrate in noisy places. ✓
• He considers it essential to review the document carefully. ✓

The same rule applies to prepositional objects:
• She shared the content with me. ✓
• She shared with me the content. ✗ (only acceptable with very long objects)
3

Verb + to-infinitive or -ing?

Some verbs are always followed by -ing:
 avoid, enjoy, finish, keep, mind, consider, suggest, practise, stop, quit

Others always take to + infinitive:
 want, decide, hope, manage, refuse, promise, plan, agree, expect, fail, need

There is no shortcut — these must be memorised.
Common mistake: avoid to waste ✗ → avoid wasting ✓
4

How + Adverb

how alone = in what way / manner — but it needs an adjective or adverb to ask about specific things:

• how long → duration or length: How long will it take?
• how much → quantity (uncountable): How much does it cost?
• how many → quantity (countable): How many people attended?
• how often → frequency: How often do you travel?
• how far → distance: How far is the office?
• how soon → time until something happens: How soon can you finish?

Common mistake: How it will take… ✗ → How long it will take… ✓

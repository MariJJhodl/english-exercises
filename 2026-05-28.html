1

Verb & Adjective + Preposition

In English, many verbs and adjectives are always followed by a fixed preposition. You cannot translate directly from Italian — the preposition must be memorised.

Common examples:
• think about (not think to)  •  used by in passive (not used from)
• good at  •  interested in  •  afraid of  •  depend on
• arrive at (a specific place: office, station)  •  important to + infinitive
2

Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a verb combined with a particle (preposition or adverb) that creates a new, often idiomatic meaning. The meaning is usually very different from the original verb.

• give up = stop trying or quit a habit  •  look up = search for information
• put off = postpone  •  turn up = arrive (often unexpectedly or late)
• carry on = continue  •  break down = stop working (machine) or lose control emotionally
• bring up = mention a topic (or raise a child)  •  find out = discover information

Read each sentence carefully — the context will help you choose the right one.
3

Choose the Formal Word

Formal writing avoids colloquial verbs and informal intensifiers. Each question shows a colloquial sentence (in italics), followed by a formal rewrite with one gap.

Key pairs to know:
• start → commence  •  show → demonstrate / indicate
• tell → notify / inform  •  help → assist
• ask for → request  •  end → conclude  •  use → employ / utilise
4

Verb + to-infinitive or -ing? (Meaning Change)

Some verbs can be followed by either form, but the meaning changes completely:

• remember to do = don’t forget to do it (future action)  →  Remember to lock the door.
• remember doing = have a memory of doing it (past)  →  I remember meeting her once.

• stop doing = quit the habit / cease  →  He stopped smoking.
• stop to do = pause in order to do something  →  He stopped to buy a coffee.

• try to do = attempt (may or may not succeed)  →  I tried to fix it but couldn’t.
• try doing = experiment with doing something  →  Try adding less salt.

• forget to do = fail to do it  →  She forgot to reply.
• forget doing = have no memory of it (past)  →  I’ll never forget meeting him.
